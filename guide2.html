Dead of Winter

This game looks complicated when it's set up, but the heart of it is simple. Here's everything you actually need to know before your first go.

The good news: you don't need to memorise anything. The group explains things as they come up, and it's totally normal to ask "wait, what does this do?" — even people who've played before do it constantly.

The story in one breath

Forget the rules for a second — here's what's happening.

It's the zombie apocalypse, and it's the middle of a freezing winter. Our little group of survivors lives together in a shared home base called the colony. Each round, we go out to places like the grocery store or the hospital to find supplies, then come home and deal with whatever went wrong that week.

Think of it like a season of a zombie TV show, where everyone at the table is one of the main characters.

Just three big ideas

If you understand these, you understand the game.

🤝 We're (mostly) a team

There's one shared goal for the whole table, like "collect enough food." If we hit it, the group succeeds. So most of the time, everyone genuinely wants to help each other.

🤫 Everyone has one little secret

You'll get a secret card with a small personal goal — something like "end the game holding two medicine cards." You quietly work on it alongside the team stuff. And here's the spicy bit: one player might have a secret goal that says "make the group fail." Might! Sometimes there's no traitor at all. Suspecting each other over nothing is half the fun.

🎲 Your turn is just: roll dice, spend dice

At the start of your turn you roll a few dice. Each die can be spent to do one thing — like search a location for supplies, or fight a zombie. That's really it. When your dice are gone, your turn's done.

What a round looks like

Don't memorise this — it'll make sense after one round. Promise.

  1. 1

    A problem of the week appears

    The game deals a "crisis" card — like "the colony needs 4 fuel this round." Everyone can chip in cards to solve it.

  2. 2

    Everyone takes their turn

    Roll your dice, then spend them: move somewhere, search for supplies, fight a zombie, or help around the colony. Ask the table what's smart — asking for advice is allowed and encouraged.

  3. 3

    Zombies shuffle closer

    At the end of the round, zombies show up at the doors. If too many pile up, they get in. This is the game's way of keeping things tense.

  4. 4

    Dinner time

    The colony eats some of the food we've gathered. Run out, and everyone's mood (called morale) drops. If morale ever hits zero, the game ends. So: food matters!

⚠️ The one genuinely scary rule

Whenever you travel between places or fight a zombie, you roll one special little die. Usually nothing happens. But sometimes you get hurt — and on one rare face, your character gets bitten, and it's game over for them (not for you — you get a new character!). It's dramatic and sad and everyone gasps. It's the best part.

Questions you might be wondering

Tap any of these to open the answer.

What if I make a mistake?

Genuinely fine. This game runs on group decisions — the table will happily help you plan every turn. Nobody plays this game solo in their head, and there's no way to secretly ruin things by accident.

Do I have to lie or act if I'm the traitor?

Barely. Most of the traitor's work is quietly doing slightly unhelpful things, not delivering Oscar speeches. And there's a good chance nobody is the traitor tonight anyway.

How long does it take?

Around 2 hours, maybe a bit more for a first game. But turns are quick and there's lots of table chat, so it doesn't drag.

What are Crossroads cards? I heard someone mention them.

Little surprise story moments. During your turn, another player is secretly holding a card that might trigger based on something you do. When it fires, they read out a short story and the group makes a choice. You can't plan for them — just enjoy them.

What should I actually do on my first turn?

An easy, safe opener: send a survivor to a location (like the grocery store) and spend a die to search for cards. Finding food or medicine is always useful, and searching never hurts anyone. Then just ask the table, "anything else I should do?"

You've got this. Grab a warm drink, pick the survivor with the best hat, and enjoy the chaos. ♥
